Part One

Problem Additional
Impact Labels

Temu needs to know about risky or sensitive cases. You tag them with the right label so the company can act fast. Trust the customer — even without proof.

The big picture

Why Labels Matter

Some cases are highly sensitive or risky — like lawsuits, injuries, or media threats. Temu has a built-in label tool on the workbench (chat, hotline, ticket agents all use it).

Simple rule: If something in the case matches a label's definition, you must tag it — even if it later gets resolved.

Trust the customer

Whether or not a high-risk claim can be verified, accept it as true and tag accordingly.

Don't ask for proof

Never proactively ask the user for evidence — unless KB, Paoding, or Q&A coaches require it.

Remember: The label tells Temu "this case is risky." It does NOT mean you skip helping the customer. Always follow user-centered service.
Label 1 of 6

Ethics & Crimes

The user mentions a crime or an act related to ethics and morals during the session.

Child Porn

Any mention of child sexual abuse material — always tag, never ask for proof.

Drug Issues

Drugs, trafficking, or selling banned substances mentioned by the customer.

Personal Safety

Murder, kidnapping, robbery, human trafficking — threats to a person's life.

Case Example

If you don't give me the refund, my husband will kill me! I'll really be dead!
Correct label: Ethics & Crimes — Personal safety issue (threat to life)
Label 2 of 6

Sensitive Identity Inquiry

Users from these groups contact CS to declare issues on behalf of an organization or individual.

Lawyer
Media / Journalist
Celebrity / KOL
Gov / Legal Authority
Legal Guardian

Important: Tag must be applied regardless of whether the user is an active employee or former employee.

I am a lawyer and a law professor. I'll file charges against Temu for stealing my money if you don't give me a refund.
Sensitive identity — Lawyer inquiry
Label 3 of 6

Fined or Sued Due to Temu

The user says they got fined or sued because of Temu. Common reasons:

Product infringement Bought banned items Items that don't meet regulations

Quick examples

  • "Customs fined me $500 because the product I bought from Temu was banned."
  • "I got sued because the item I resold was a trademark infringement."
  • "The local authority fined me because the product didn't meet safety rules."
Key point: The fine or lawsuit must be caused by Temu's product, service, or merchant — not an unrelated issue. Trust the customer's claim.
Label 4 of 6

Legal Action / Gov Complaint

The user has resolved or intends to resolve the issue through public authorities (government, regulatory, judicial, or law enforcement).

High Risk
Mentions specific authorities, has clear timelines, supporting evidence (receipts, case numbers, emails), formal legal docs
Medium Risk
Only expresses intention to seek help from authorities — without specific details, evidence, or timelines

Upgrade Rule

In medium risk cases, upgrade to high risk if the customer also requests Temu company information (legal representative, registered address, etc.).

High Risk
I told you I called the police yesterday! Here's my police record. I just want my money back!
Medium Risk
I'll get a lawyer.
Label 5 of 6

Human Injury

The user has experienced injuries or severe illness due to Temu — directly or indirectly. Suicide or self-harm due to Temu also counts as human injury.

High Risk
Death, hospitalization, miscarriage, self-harm/suicide with supporting evidence (medical records, police reports, photos, third-party docs)
Medium Risk
Physical injury or health issues that don't meet the high-risk threshold, OR self-harm/suicide expressions without supporting evidence

Upgrade to High Risk if any apply:

  • Person has sensitive identity (elderly, children, pregnant, disabled, serious health issues)
  • Customer also filed/intends to file gov complaints or legal action
  • Customer shows intent for external complaint (non-government)
What counts as "substantial impact": Visible damage (skin breakdown, redness, swelling), illness (nausea, dizziness, allergies, psychological issues). Discomfort alone is NOT human injury.
Label 6 of 6

Property Damage

The user experienced property damage due to Temu issues — directly or indirectly. Usually refers to damage of the customer's private property.

High Risk
Claimed amount is ≥ USD 600
Medium Risk
Claimed amount is < USD 600 / no specified amount
Upgrade if…
Sensitive identity, gov complaint, OR external complaint intent

Is it property damage? (Yes / No)

  • Headphone broken? No — only the Temu product itself, not other property.
  • Shoes uncomfortable? No — discomfort is subjective, not injury.
  • Leaking pen stained other items in same package? No — those items are all from Temu in one shipment, so it's an after-sales "product damaged" case.
Label 6 — non-gov

External Complaint (Non-Gov)

The user intends to submit / has submitted a complaint or exposure material — and may provide evidence or timelines.

High Risk
Submitted complaint to high-impact external channel (TV, newspaper, executive, KOL) with supporting evidence
Medium Risk
Submitted complaint to high-impact channel but no evidence, OR intention to a specific channel, OR no channel but clear timeline
Low Risk
General intention, no specific channel, no clear timeline

Upgrade & Downgrade rules

  • Upgrade: Traditional media (TV, newspaper) carry higher risk than low-sensitivity platforms → upgrade to high risk.
  • Downgrade: Lower-sensitivity platforms (social media, BBB, Trustpilot) → downgrade to low risk, even with evidence.
Tagging rules

Priority & Combo Rules

When multiple sensitive issues appear in one case, follow this priority order (lowest → highest):

1 External complaint (non-gov)
↑
2 Legal action / complaint to gov authorities
↑
3 Fined or sued due to Temu issues
↑
4 Sensitive identity inquiry
↑
5 Ethics & Crimes

Special priority notes

  • If case involves Human Injury OR Property Damage + another sensitive issue → use the "Human injury / Property damage" label.
  • If both human injury AND property damage → use the "Human injury" label.
  • Medium-risk legal action + high-risk external complaint → label as external complaint (non-gov) high risk.
  • High-risk legal action + high-risk external complaint → label as legal action (gov) high risk.
Rule of thumb: Always trust the customer's claim. If a high-sensitivity issue is mentioned at any point in the chat, review the whole interaction and apply the right tag — even if the issue is later resolved.
External complaints

Handling Process

Step 1 — Definition. The customer claims they used / will use non-Temu channels to complain or spread negative info that harms Temu's reputation. Includes: gov/legal complaints, external high-impact channels (media, social, execs), or family/friend with special identity (lawyer, journalist, influencer).
Step 2 — Identify the customer. If they state a sensitive identity, modify the additional label. If reporting to Q&A coach is needed, use TPD keyword "External complaint or legal action". Regular customers follow normal SOP.
Step 3 — Did the complaint already occur? If they clearly state they've already acted → "Already occurred". Everything else → "Not yet occurred".
Step 3.1 — Already occurred. Modify label, report to Q&A coach with TPD keyword if needed.
Step 3.2 — Not yet occurred. Try to resolve per KB. If after your solution the customer mentions external complaint again, treat as "Already occurred" and follow that process.
Summary — 3 things to remember: 1) User-centered service (trust even without proof). 2) Always choose the correct impact label. 3) Empathy first — pacify and provide best solutions.

Edge case

Ticket Outside SRS Scope?

What if you receive a ticket that doesn't fit any SRS group? For example: an external complaint medium-risk ticket, or a case the customer escalated to PIPD?

Step 1: Confirm with VM / Q&A coach — what is the correct additional impact, and does the ticket need to be transferred?
If transfer is needed: Edit the correct additional label, then request the in-house team to transfer. (The system may auto-transfer to the right skill group.)
If no transfer needed: Proceed with handling the ticket according to the current regulations.
Tip: When in doubt, ask the Q&A coach first. Never silently close or re-route a ticket without confirmation.
Spot official emails

Email Domain Verification

Key rule: Keywords + Country code domain suffixes. Most cases show both. Some show only one. If unsure, report to Q&A coach.

Keywords to spot

  • Organization → .or (Japan/Korea), .org (international)
  • Government → .gov / .go (Asia) / .gob (Spanish) / .gouv (France)
  • Consumer → consumer / consumo (Italian)
  • Protection → pro / proc / procon (Latin America)
  • Protest → protest (EN) / proteste (Portuguese)

Country suffixes

.it — Italy .kr — Korea .br — Brazil .jp — Japan .be — Belgium .pl — Poland .at — Austria .pe — Peru .cz — Czech Republic .co — Colombia .fr — France .ph — Philippines

How to report to Q&A coach: More actions → Create a Q&A ticket → Report to Q&A coach → Others.

6 sections to look for

Standardized Email Format

Official institutional or legal complaint emails follow a fixed, structured layout with clear section headers — not plain unformatted text.

Typical structure

1. Case Number
2. Complainant Information
3. Statement of Authority
4. Facts / Complaint Details
5. Legal Demands / Required Actions
6. Evidence Attached

What to do for org/institutional emails

1. Identify as org/institutional ticket
2. Create legal ticket via Ticket Paoding
3. Search "Law firms" keywords & select nodes
4. Confirm info & create legal ticket
5. Send email to guide
6. Tag ticket & close per KB
High-risk subject examples: [Case No. XXX] Formal Complaint from [Customer Name] · [Case No. XXX] Complaint Notice - [Institutional Name] · Formal Complaint: [Core Content] (Case No. XXX, [Customer Name])

Part 3.1

Return Label Basics

A return label helps customers send packages back through a designated carrier. There are two types:

Provided by Temu

  • Carrier: Temu
  • Auto-generated after after-sales request
  • Available instantly

Provided by Merchant

  • Carrier: Merchant-partnered
  • Uploaded by merchant after after-sales request
  • Timeline varies (see KB 3.2)

Which ticket to create?

Decide based on the label type (Temu vs Merchant), NOT the warehouse type. Temu label → "After-Sales Related Ticket". Merchant label → "Merchant verification ticket".

Common issue

Return Label Generation Failed

Follow the "Return label generation failure reason" shown on the Workbench:

System Issue
Submit information ticket "After-sales Related Ticket — Return label generation failed". If it times out, BPO submits a Q&A ticket.
Name / Postal / Address Issues
Within 24h → ask user to wait. Over 24h → If order ≤ USD 50: offer full Exceptional Compensation refund. If > USD 50 or EC balance insufficient: negotiate 30% refund without return; if refused, guide user to self-return.
Package Overweight
Explain & ask if user accepts split returns. If yes — free label (KB 2.72). If no — negotiate 30% refund; if refused, guide self-return.
Home Pickup Scheduling Issue
Guide user to cancel current return request, start a new one, and pick another pickup time.
US Site

Home Pickup & Self-Return

Small Item ≤ 150 lbs

  • Tracking starts with "1Z"
  • Residential address: $7.99 fee (deducted from refund)
  • Commercial address: no fee
  • Print label & stick on package
  • Free returns for unused orders

Large Item > 150 lbs

  • Tracking starts with "3"
  • If front-end shows "Select home pickup address" → no fee
  • No need to print label (tracking # may be needed)
  • Restrictions on free returns for unused orders
  • Address must be in reachable postal code

Self-Return types

Basic: No platform label, no free return shipping — user pays.
Fallback: Platform label exists but item too heavy / location out of coverage.
Off-system: Tier 2 offers self-return solution outside standard process.
Reimbursement methods: Credit (all sites) · PayPal (USA, Japan, Canada, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, UK, AU, NZ) · Bank transfer (USA). Risk control users get reimbursement after warehouse confirms; C4-5 users can get advance reimbursement in transit.
Part 3.2

Account & Payment Issues

Order Issues

Cannot find the order: Customer can't see a specific order in their account.

Payment Issues

Payment failed · Duplicate charge · Bank card theft (unauthorized charges with no matching order).

Account Issues

Unauthorized orders / account logged in by someone else: Order in account not placed by user, or security issues (phone/email/password changed, account theft, lost device).

Identify the situation — 2 questions

1. Was payment authorized? Customer said "I paid" → YES. Customer said "I didn't pay", "Someone used my card", "It wasn't me" → NO.

2. Can the order be found under current UID? Tier 2 manually checks the Order tab — yes/no. (Even if order is in a linked account, still mark NO.)

Step-by-step

Handling Procedures

Cannot Find Order

Step 1: Search by payment / currency / amount / time, OR by tracking number.
Step 2: If found in current UID → send order card, end.
Step 3: If guest account / different account → follow KB 4.2.5 cross-account procedures.

Bank Card Theft

Step 1: Create Information Ticket [Risk Control Related Ticket / Appeal for Unknown Payment Charges]. Required: amount, time, channel, last 4 digits of card. Optional: address, UID/email, CS note, screenshot.
Step 2: Check ticket result → not unauthorized / confirmed unauthorized / uncertain → follow KB script per result.

Unauthorized Orders / Account Logged In

Step 1: Create Information Ticket [Account Related Ticket / Account Security Scenarios] — problem type: "Orders not placed by myself."
Step 2: Check result → Situation 1 (not theft) inform customer per KB · Situation 2 (theft confirmed) help recover account + set 2FA · Situation 3 (stolen + possible unauthorized order) PO will be refunded.
Common Tier 2 mistakes: Not filling required blanks correctly, attaching wrong screenshot, screenshot doesn't prove Temu charge, vague transaction time, creating info ticket without customer proof.
Part 3.3

Report CS / Seller

Report Customer Service

Definition: Customer clearly requests to file a complaint against CS.

Script (CS report):

"We've noted your feedback about customer service and will review and optimize our communication and service experience accordingly."

Script (CS at fault):

"We regret the experience didn't meet your expectations. As a valued customer, we've added a €10 credit to your Temu account."

Report Seller

  • Selling a specific product → KB 4.1.2.7
  • Order-related issue (logistics/after-sales) → send apology script
  • Merchant insulted user → Paoding [Sellers] → [File complaint against merchant/CS]. Check definitions: abusive, threatening, mocking language.

Insult verified: Send apology script + inform of regulatory actions. Not verified: Apologize + offer to monitor seller (20% coupon as EC if user insists).

When cases stall

Stuck at One Point

SRS cases can take days or even up to a month — but customers are always in a hurry. Here's how to handle the pressure:

DOs

  • If inhouse has intervened and no solution yet, contact in-house Q&A / VM and check if a reassurance email can be sent.
  • If Q&A ticket times out, urge the Q&A ticket.
  • If no Q&A coach/in-house guidance is needed but the user keeps urging while verification is in progress, contact in-house to determine how to proceed.

DON'Ts

  • Do NOT send 2 consecutive reassurance emails.
  • If inhouse has intervened: do NOT send reassurance email without approval, do NOT reply to user before getting instructions.
  • Do NOT apply "no need to respond" — this may cause you to miss updates since no countdown will be triggered.
Accuracy & closure

Locate & Close Tickets

How to locate complicated situations accurately

  1. 1. Review all workbench info: chat history (ticket creation + most recent message; AI summaries are reference only), hotline call text transcripts (listen to recording if unsure), complete ticket flow (Q&A tickets, info tickets, first-line notes), activity / payment / after-sales / logistics / order item details.
  2. 2. Organize issues & demands chronologically. If timeline is long, list requests' development.
  3. 3. Refer to issues & demands review, use KB or Paoding to find solutions one by one. Verify with relevant department if there are inconsistencies.
  4. 4. Make sure your email response doesn't miss any issues.

Closure ticket requirements

Marking "solved-yes" directly closes the ticket. Some tickets require VM / Q&A coach instructions before closing:

Risk_Gov (Ethics/Fined/Legal): YES, Q&A confirmation required.
Risk_Identity: YES, Q&A confirmation required.
Risk_NonGov (high risk): NO — but if case has valid proof (relevant proof EC, lawsuit documents), don't close directly. Report to inhouse first.
